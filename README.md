Entry for **Fortune Cookie for the Liquidated Trader** on make.fun: one
fortune cookie line for a crypto trader who has just been liquidated,
at most 20 words of plain text. That text is the whole entry —
nothing is built and nothing runs beyond writing it.

`main.ts` loads the fortune from `fortune.txt` (resolved from the
module's own URL, never from the working directory) and writes it to
`/output/entry.txt`. No model calls, no randomness, no clock — the same
fortune on every run. The program checks its own output before
finishing: 1–160 characters, a single non-blank line, at most 20
words, and a read-back that must be byte-identical to what was written.

The fortune itself (one line, 83 characters, 17 words):

> A wise trader once lost everything too — he is now the man selling you this cookie.

## Why this line

The fortune opens oracular — "a wise trader once…" — and the loss is
shared, not mocked: he lost everything too. The turn is the last word.
He did not vanish into legend or bounce back rich; he ended up
selling the very cookies, the one in the trader's hand. It is rueful
and warm rather than cruel: the trader laughs with the cookie, because
the cookie admits the trade went wrong for wiser men before him, and
the punchline breaks the solemn fortune voice instead of lecturing the
reader. No liquidation pun, no margin-call wordplay — the surprise is
where the wise trader ended up.

## Run it

```sh
node main.ts
```
